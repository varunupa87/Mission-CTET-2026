## Content (20 Qs)

**Food**
- Sources of food (plant/animal), components of food (nutrients), cleaning food (hygiene/safety).

**Materials**
- Materials of daily use — classifying by properties (hardness, transparency, conductivity, solubility etc.).

**The World of the Living**
- Plants, animals, human body basics, life processes.

**Moving Things, People and Ideas**
- Force and motion of objects; conceptually links physical motion with movement of people/ideas (trade, migration).

**How Things Work**
- **Electric current and circuits**: series circuit — single path, so one break stops current everywhere.
- **Magnets**: poles, attraction/repulsion, magnetic materials.

**Natural Phenomena**
- Light (reflection, refraction — e.g. rainbow formation), sound, heat in everyday contexts.

**Natural Resources**
- Water, air, soil; renewable vs. non-renewable; conservation focus (e.g. why we shouldn't waste water).

## Pedagogical Issues (10 Qs)

- **Nature & Structure of Sciences**: science = an evolving, evidence-based way of understanding the world — not a fixed set of facts.
- **Natural Science/Aims & objectives**: what school science education is trying to achieve.
- **Approaches/Integrated Approach**: connecting science meaningfully across related concepts/subjects, not teaching in isolation.
- **Observation/Experiment/Discovery (Method of Science)**: inquiry-based, hands-on learning is preferred over lecture — e.g. letting students *test* "do heavier objects fall faster?" themselves.
- **Innovation**: bringing new methods/materials into teaching, not sticking rigidly to the textbook.
- **Text Material/Aids**: role of good textbooks and teaching aids.
- **Evaluation — cognitive/psycho-motor/affective**:
  - Cognitive = knowledge/understanding (written tests).
  - Psycho-motor = practical/hands-on skill (e.g. correctly setting up a circuit).
  - Affective = attitude/interest/curiosity toward science.
- **Remedial Teaching**: target specific misconceptions (e.g. "heavier objects fall faster").
